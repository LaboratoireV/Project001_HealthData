Synthetic Alberta emergency & inpatient data.

Project 001 generates fictional NACRS and DAD records so two linked questions can be studied without a single real patient: whether triage acuity and the time before a disposition decision predict hospitalization, and, among linked admissions, whether acuity and emergency stay predict inpatient length of stay. Seven consulting memos carry the work from raw fields to final findings.

The research

Two questions, studied in order

Both questions use CTAS, the Canadian Triage and Acuity Scale (levels 1–5, where level 1 is most urgent). The first question decides who is hospitalized; the second follows those admissions into the inpatient record.

Question 1 · Memo A03

Hospitalization — yes or no?

Among emergency visits, do CTAS and the time before the disposition decision affect whether the patient is hospitalized?

Population
Emergency (NACRS) visits
Exposure
Pre-disposition stay pre_disposition_los_min and ctas_level
Outcome
Hospitalization admitted_flag (1 = yes, 0 = no)
Model
Logistic regression
Appendix
Total emergency stay los_min is a comparison, not the primary exposure
Question 2 · Memo A05

Inpatient length of stay — how long?

Among hospitalized visits that link to a DAD record, do CTAS and total emergency stay affect inpatient length of stay in days?

The data

Two synthetic Alberta sources

Every value is randomly generated. The records imitate the structure — the record grain, field families, and linkage — of two real Alberta administrative sources, without using any real patient information.

NACRS

Emergency & ambulatory care

National Ambulatory Care Reporting System. Here, one row is an emergency department visit — arrival, triage, disposition timing, and whether the patient was admitted.

30,000Records
34Variables
1Row = one ED visit
DAD

Inpatient care

Discharge Abstract Database. Here, one row is an inpatient stay, including the derived length of stay. Question 2 uses the subset linked from a hospitalized NACRS visit.

60,000Records
51Variables
1Row = one stay
